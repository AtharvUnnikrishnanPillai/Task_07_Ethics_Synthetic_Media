# Synthetic Media Policy

Organization: Hypothetical Campus Data Literacy Lab
Policy owner: Lab Director
Status: Proposed policy for an academic exercise
Review cycle: Every six months and after a material incident

## 1. Purpose and Scope

The lab may use synthetic media to explain verified analytical
findings and improve access to instruction.

This policy applies to staff, contractors, faculty collaborators,
and student assistants producing audio or video for the lab,
including prototypes and externally hosted lessons.

Every released output must have an accurate script, documented
rights, accessible disclosure, and an accountable human reviewer.

## 2. Permitted Uses

The lab may use licensed generic voices and fictional presenters for:

- Narration of verified educational explanations.
- Accessible versions of approved instructional text.
- Clearly identified fictional classroom simulations.
- Translations reviewed by a person competent in both the language
  and subject matter.

A generic voice must not be presented as a particular instructor
or student.

An identifiable adult staff member’s synthetic likeness may be
considered only when a generic alternative cannot meet a documented
instructional or accessibility need. The director and university
privacy reviewer must approve the justification and consent record
before source material is uploaded.

## 3. Prohibited Uses

The lab must not:

- Replicate students, minors, unwilling adults, or public figures.
- Fabricate endorsements, quotations, interviews, or events.
- Imply approval by a researcher or instructor without that approval.
- Conceal material uncertainty or fabricate findings.
- Generate intimate, humiliating, or deliberately deceptive depictions.
- Use synthetic identities for authentication or requests for money.
- Use synthetic delivery for emergency alerts, individualized grading,
  disciplinary decisions, admissions decisions, political persuasion,
  or legal or medical instructions.

Disclosure and educational intent do not override these prohibitions.

## 4. Consent Workflow

For a permitted identifiable adult representation, the producer must
obtain written authorization before collecting or uploading source
voice or likeness material.

Authorization must specify:

- The represented individual.
- Purpose and exact approved script.
- Audience and distribution channels.
- Vendor and storage arrangements.
- Duration and retention period.
- Whether a reusable model will be created.
- Permitted reuse and revocation procedures.

Consent must be voluntary and separate from employment, grading,
participation, or access to services. A generic alternative must
be available.

Authorization expires after 90 days or the approved use ends,
whichever occurs first. Changed scripts, channels, or purposes
require renewed authorization.

On revocation, staff must acknowledge receipt within one business
day, stop new production immediately, and withdraw controlled copies
within two business days. Staff must request removal of external
copies without promising complete internet removal.

Source recordings and reusable models must be deleted within
30 days after the authorized use ends or consent is revoked,
unless a legally required hold applies.

Consent and deletion records must be retained for three years in
restricted storage.

## 5. Disclosure Standards

Every synthetic audio must begin with a plain spoken disclosure.
Audio longer than 60 seconds must repeat a brief notice at least once.

Every separately released excerpt must include its own disclosure.

Videos must display a readable synthetic-media notice throughout
synthetic representations and include matching captions and
transcript labels.

Filenames must include AI_generated or synthetic. Player pages and
transcripts must identify the method, reviewer, date, and canonical
source.

Authorized replicas must explicitly state that the media was
generated and is not a recording of the person speaking.

Metadata alone is insufficient disclosure.

## 6. Provenance and Production Records

The producer must retain:

- Approved script and cited sources.
- Factual checks.
- Tool and model identifiers actually exposed.
- Generation settings and dates.
- Edit history.
- Rights and consent records.
- Disclosure text.
- Final files and hashes.
- Review and release approvals.

Use signed content credentials where supported by an approved tool,
and validate them after final export.

Ordinary tags and unsigned hash lists must not be described as
authenticated provenance.

If signed credentials are unavailable, the IT liaison may approve
a documented exception for generic, low-risk educational narration.
The exception requires in-band disclosure, a controlled canonical
copy, and complete review records. It cannot authorize prohibited
uses or identifiable replicas.

## 7. Detector Evidence

When detection is used, record the provider, exposed version,
test date, input filename and hash, scores, and available report.

Do not describe a confidence score as measured accuracy.

A positive result does not prove impersonation or waive disclosure.
A negative result does not prove human origin.

No disciplinary or reputational accusation may rely solely on a
detector score. Uncertainty must be investigated through source
records and accountable human review.

## 8. Review and Approval

Before generation:

1. The producer submits purpose, audience, script, sources, proposed
   voice or presenter, rights record, channels, and expected volume.
2. The faculty reviewer checks claims, units, numbers, and limitations.
3. The instructional designer checks accessibility and attribution.
4. The privacy reviewer checks identifiable representations and
   vendor handling where applicable.

Before release, someone other than the producer must review the
complete final output, compare it with the approved script, and
verify pronunciation, disclosure, and export integrity.

The director approves the final version and distribution channel.

Changes after approval require renewed review. Production must be
deferred when reviewer capacity cannot support complete checking.

## 9. Refusal Conditions

The lab must refuse a project when:

- Claims cannot be verified.
- Necessary consent is absent or pressured.
- Vendor handling conflicts with authorized retention.
- The intended use is prohibited.
- Reasonable disclosure cannot prevent misleading attribution.
- Human delivery is necessary for consequential communication.
- Required review or disclosure cannot be completed.

If an export loses required safeguards, hold release, repair and
retest, or use human narration or written text.

The director cannot waive prohibited-use categories.

## 10. Incident Response

Anyone identifying false claims, misattribution, missing disclosure,
or unauthorized reuse must notify the director promptly.

The director must:

1. Record the incident and assign an owner within one business day.
2. Stop controlled distribution immediately when urgent harm exists.
3. Preserve restricted evidence before removing controlled copies.
4. Notify affected represented individuals and relevant university
   offices within one business day of verification.
5. Publish a correction where the original audience can find it.
6. Request downstream correction or removal and track the outcome.
7. Complete a cause review within ten business days.

Applicable legal deadlines take priority over internal targets.

## 11. Audit and Enforcement

Maintain a restricted release register and a public canonical index
of approved lessons.

Monthly, the IT liaison must audit all identifiable-representation
exceptions and a random 10% of generic releases, with at least five
reviewed where volume permits.

Sampling supplements complete prepublication review; it does not
replace it.

Track missing records, disclosure failures, correction times,
revocation response times, and unresolved incidents.

Suspend production privileges when required records or reviews are
missing. Refer deliberate misuse through existing university
procedures.

## 12. Policy Limitations

Read [Policy Limitations](Policy_Limitations.md) alongside this policy.
The policy does not guarantee prevention of misuse or authorize
deployment without local operational and legal review.
