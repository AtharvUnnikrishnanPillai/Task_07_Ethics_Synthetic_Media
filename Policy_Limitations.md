# Policy Limitations and Stress Tests

## Purpose

This document explains where the proposed Campus Data Literacy Lab
policy could fail despite its safeguards.

The policy has not been field-tested in a real organization.

## 1. Well-Intentioned Excerpting

A staff member might repost an approved 20-second excerpt without
adding a disclosure, assuming the original recording’s notice is
sufficient.

The excerpt rule addresses this behavior, but enforcement depends
on training, a channel inventory, and awareness of redistributed
copies.

Private sharing can remain outside the lab’s visibility.

## 2. Inattentive Review

A reviewer may miss an incorrectly pronounced number, an omitted
qualification, or a misleading emphasis.

Independent review reduces unchecked production but cannot guarantee
attention or expertise.

Reviewers need time, clear criteria, access to the source script,
and a correction process.

## 3. Consent under Unequal Power

An employee may feel pressured to authorize a replica even when a
form states that consent is voluntary.

A generic alternative and privacy review help, but organizational
culture may still discourage refusal.

A signature is evidence of authorization, not conclusive evidence
that the decision was free from pressure.

## 4. Deliberate Misuse

An outsider can crop notices, imitate the lab’s branding, or circulate
a fabricated clip.

The policy cannot control that person. Canonical sources and public
corrections help only when recipients encounter and use them.

The lab cannot guarantee removal of downloaded or privately shared
copies.

## 5. Detection Errors

A detector may miss synthetic audio or incorrectly flag human speech.

The Task 6 screenshot concerns one likely recording and does not
establish general detector reliability.

Using a score as proof could create false accusations. Detection
must remain supporting evidence rather than the sole basis for a
consequential decision.

## 6. Provenance Gaps

Content credentials may be unavailable or incompatible across tools.
Exports and distribution systems may fail to preserve them.

A valid credential does not establish that the content is true or
that consent was ethically obtained.

Missing credentials do not establish that content is false.

## 7. Translation and Accessibility

An accurate original can become misleading through translation,
pronunciation, caption errors, or inaccessible disclosure.

A reviewer must understand the language, subject, and final medium.
Approval of one version does not automatically approve another.

## 8. Production Pressure

Deadlines may encourage staff to reuse approvals, sample outputs
instead of reviewing every release, or leave production records
incomplete.

The policy requires production to match review capacity, but that
requirement depends on management providing sufficient time and staff.

## 9. Reliance on Good Faith

The policy depends on people reporting mistakes, retaining records,
and honoring refusals.

That dependence is a real limitation. Independent review, audits,
protected reporting, and meaningful enforcement reduce the risk
without eliminating it.

## Residual Risk

Residual risk is acceptable only for the narrow, low-risk educational
uses covered by the policy, with funded review and correction capacity.

The policy does not justify emergency communication, impersonation,
or other prohibited uses merely because disclosure is present.

Its effectiveness must be evaluated through actual incidents,
audit findings, staff feedback, and periodic revision.
