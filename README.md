# Task 07 — Ethics and Synthetic Media

Author: Atharv Unnikrishnan Pillai

## Project Overview

This project examines the ethical implications of synthetic audio and
proposes an operational policy for a hypothetical university Campus
Data Literacy Lab.

The analysis builds on Task 6, which includes two ElevenLabs Roger
recordings and an AI-audio detection screenshot. It considers how the
same production method could create different harms depending on
truth, consent, context, and scale.

## Organizational Setting

The Campus Data Literacy Lab is a hypothetical university teaching
unit producing short analytical lessons, narrated explanations, and
accessible transcripts.

Its staff includes a director, instructional designers, a faculty
subject reviewer, student assistants, and an IT liaison. A university
privacy reviewer supports projects involving identifiable people.

This is a proposed policy for an academic exercise, not an adopted
Syracuse University policy.

## Central Finding

A detector identifying audio as synthetic does not establish whether
its claims are true, whether its use is authorized, or whether its
presentation misleads an audience.

Responsible use therefore requires source verification, appropriate
permission, clear disclosure, and accountable human review.

## Reflection

The Task 6 evidence illustrates a gap between identifying synthetic
content and determining whether its use is responsible. The supplied
detector screenshot reports a 95% AI score, but that score cannot
answer questions about factual accuracy, consent, or attribution.

Personal listening reactions to the new ElevenLabs recordings have
not yet been documented. No experience of being fooled or preference
between those recordings is claimed.
